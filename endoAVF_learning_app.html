Endovascular arteriovenous fistula (endoAVF) creation is a minimally invasive, percutaneous alternative to surgical fistula creation for patients who require long-term hemodialysis access.

Background
Why AVFs Are Preferred

Arteriovenous fistulas are the gold standard for vascular access in end-stage renal disease. Compared to central venous catheters (CVCs) and arteriovenous grafts, AVFs offer superior long-term patency with far lower infection and reintervention rates. Traditionally formed by a surgeon, AVFs can now be created percutaneously using two FDA-cleared systems, opening the door to patients who prefer or require a non-surgical approach.

A crucial anatomical requirement for both devices is the perforator vein — a communicating vein in the forearm that connects the deep and superficial venous systems. Without it, arterialized blood cannot reach the superficial cephalic or basilic veins needed for needle cannulation during dialysis.

The Two Devices
WavelinQ vs. Ellipsys

WavelinQ (Becton Dickinson)

  • Fistula LocationRadial artery–radial vein, or ulnar artery–ulnar vein
  • AccessBrachial artery and vein via micropuncture
  • MechanismMagnetic catheter alignment + RF electrode pulse (0.7 sec)
  • Min Vessel Diameter≥2 mm; separation <2 mm
  • 12-mo Primary Patency~69%

Ellipsys (Medtronic)

  • Fistula LocationProximal radial artery (PRA) + deep communicating vein (perforator)
  • AccessBasilic/cephalic vein, navigated to perforator
  • MechanismSingle-needle direct puncture + radiofrequency thermal energy
  • Min Vessel Diameter≥2 mm; separation <1.5 mm
  • 12-mo Primary Patency~54–86%
Procedure & Follow-Up
What Happens Before, During & After

Patient Selection

Best for patients avoiding surgery or unsuitable for radiocephalic fistula. Contraindications include thrombosed veins and flow-limiting arterial calcification.

Vessel Mapping

Preprocedural ultrasound confirms vessel diameters, proximity of target vessels, and perforator vein patency — mandatory before either device is used.

Anesthesia

Local anesthesia with moderate sedation (midazolam + fentanyl). Brachial plexus block can improve vessel dilation and patient comfort during RF pulse.

Maturation Monitoring

Doppler ultrasound every ~2 weeks. Flow ≥500–600 mL/min is the target. Maturation ranges from 26–137 days depending on the device used.

Cannulation Tips

Tourniquet required; shallow needle angle (25–35°). Diagram the cannulation zone for technicians since there is no scar or skin protrusion to guide them.

Complications

Hematoma (9%), thrombosis (9%), pseudoaneurysm (3%), arterial dissection (1%). Overall rate ~5.46%; most are minor and treatable.

Outcomes
EndoAVF vs. Surgical AVF: Key Comparisons

Primary Patency: One retrospective study showed 86% at 12 months with endoAVF versus 61% with surgical AVF — a statistically significant difference.

Intervention Rate: Dramatically lower with endoAVF (0.59–0.74 per patient year) versus surgical AVF (3.43–7.22 per patient year). Two-year re-intervention rates were 17% vs. 36%, respectively.

Cost: EndoAVF is projected to save $11,240–$13,389 per patient compared to surgical AVF, largely driven by reduced reintervention rates.

Limitations: Evidence remains limited. Prospective, multicenter randomized controlled trials are still needed to definitively characterize the indications for each approach.
